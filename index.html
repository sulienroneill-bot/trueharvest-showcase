Telling the authentic story of running multimodal VQA to catch regressions and automating the fix.

Buildings in public update: Why we ran a Visual QA (VQA) audit on our food transparency co-pilot, and what happened when it failed. A few days ago, I shared the initial concept for TrueHarvest—an in-store grocery co-pilot built to cut through grocery aisle greenwashing and verify conglomerate ownership with a single scan. After finishing the first three sprints (design tokens, navigation, initial swap cards, and directory), I could have declared victory. The code compiled, the database was rich, and the UI worked. Instead, I decided to test our frontend with an autonomous Multimodal Visual QA (VQA) audit using Gemini in AI Studio, evaluating our screen recordings against our production design specification. THE AUDIT VERDICT: FAIL. The multimodal inspector caught several major UX regressions that a simple unit test would never catch: • An uncollapsed standards banner was taking up 60% of the primary fold, pushing the scanner below the fold. • "Card inception": colored bordered boxes nested inside other colored bordered boxes. • A revenue formatting bug displaying $12M brand sales as "$0.0B". • Missing 393px safe-area top clearance on modern phone hardware cutouts. Instead of hand-patching each bug, we translated the VQA audit findings directly into a structured remediation roadmap: — Sprint 4: in-store ergonomics (44px collapsible drawer, embedded 32px mini barcode trigger, un-nested swap card with circular score gauges). — Sprint 5: price delta transparency cards, 3-tier brand directory architecture, and 393px horizontal overflow guards. This is the real power of modern AI-assisted workflows: not chaotic "vibe coding", but systematic verification where multimodal models audit the visual outputs, and modular sprints execute the fixes directly to disk.
